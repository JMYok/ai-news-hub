### No Priors: AI for Atoms — Liam Fedus (Co-Founder @ Periodic Labs)

**The Takeaway:** The AI revolution is moving from bits to atoms — connecting language models to physical experiments in materials science, chemistry, and engineering. This requires a fundamentally different kind of AI system, one that's grounded in experimental data and can operate in closed loops with real-world validation. The implications are staggering: if software engineering could be automated through recursive self-improvement, the same could happen for physical science and engineering — with equal transformative potential.

Fedus, who co-created ChatGPT at OpenAI and previously worked on some of the first trillion-parameter models at Google Brain, is now building Periodic Labs as an "AI foundation lab for atoms." His thesis: you're not going to see the same kind of acceleration in science unless you start connecting AI systems to the physical world. Science requires experiments, not just thinking. And the AI technology of 2022 was simply too weak to enable this — but test-time inference, reliable tool use, and better reasoning have finally changed that equation.

One of the most striking observations: the data quality problem in scientific fields is severe. When his team looked at reported material properties in literature, the values spanned many orders of magnitude — training an ML system on that just models the distribution, not the ground truth. Experimental data with active closed-loop feedback is what closes that gap. The future he envisions: AI systems that generate matter, not just text — with implications for semiconductors, aerospace, energy, and beyond.

"The physical sciences, physical engineering will have a very similar [scaling] property... bringing that mindset and scale to physics." — Liam Fedus
