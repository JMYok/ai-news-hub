### Why Traditional Benchmarks Fail Modern AI Models with OpenAI Research Scientist Noam Brown

**The Takeaway**

Traditional AI benchmarks are fundamentally broken for modern models because they ignore test-time compute—a factor that can make the same model perform wildly differently depending on how long it "thinks."

Noam Brown, one of OpenAI's leading research scientists and a pioneer in AI reasoning, sits down with host Sarah Ferreira on the *No Priors* podcast to explain why the industry's evaluation methods are dangerously outdated. If you care about understanding AI capability progress—or making informed decisions about which models to use—this conversation is essential.

Brown's core argument is straightforward but counterintuitive: **"The capability of the model is a function of how much money you put into it, basically."** Give GPT-4 $10 versus $10,000 in compute, and you'll get dramatically different results. Yet current benchmarks don't account for this variable at all.

He points to GPT-5.5's release as a case study. Initial skepticism about the model's improvement evaporated once people actually used it—but the benchmark grids showed only marginal gains. The reason? 5.5 is simply more *efficient*: it achieves what 5.4 achieves in less thinking time. Once you control for compute, the improvement becomes clear.

The problem is that performance plateaus are now extremely far out. Modern models can productively "think" for weeks before hitting diminishing returns, making comprehensive evaluation impractical. Brown suggests the solution: either set explicit compute budgets for benchmarks or plot performance *as a function of* test-time compute.

He's also skeptical of "benchmark maxing"—the practice of running models multiple times or using scaffolding to inflate scores. It looks impressive on paper but obscures whether models are actually improving or just being evaluated more aggressively.

For practitioners, Brown's pragmatic advice: create your own evaluation set based on real tasks you care about. He uses building poker bots as his personal benchmark—a domain with enough complexity and few shortcuts.

**中文摘要**

传统AI基准测试已经过时，因为它们忽视了“推理时计算”这一关键因素——现代模型投入的计算资源越多，性能提升就越大。

OpenAI首席研究科学家诺姆·布朗（Noam Brown）在*No Priors*播客中与主持人莎拉·费雷拉对谈，揭示了AI评估方法的深层缺陷。布朗是推理时计算扩展领域的先驱，他的观点对任何关注AI能力发展的人都至关重要。

布朗的核心论点直击要害：“模型的能力本质上取决于你投入多少资金。”同样的模型，给它10美元或10000美元的算力，性能表现会截然不同。然而，现有基准测试完全忽视了这一变量。

他以GPT-5.5的发布为例。最初业界对其改进持怀疑态度，但用户实际体验后疑虑消散——然而基准测试图表只显示了微小提升。原因在于：5.5的效率更高，能用更少的“思考时间”达到5.4的效果。控制算力变量后，改进幅度一目了然。

真正的困境在于：性能曲线现在趋于极端。现代模型可以“思考”数周才触及瓶颈，这让全面评估变得不切实际。布朗提出解决方案：要么为基准测试设定明确的算力预算，要么将性能绘制为推理时计算的函数图。

他对“基准刷榜”同样持谨慎态度——通过多次运行或外部框架来提升分数，虽然纸面好看，却掩盖了真实改进。

对从业者，布朗的实用建议是：基于实际任务创建自己的评估体系。他用扑克机器人开发作为个人基准——这个领域足够复杂，难以取巧。
