### Google DeepMind's Logan Kilpatrick: Why the Model Eats the Harness

**The Takeaway:** Google is betting that the agent harness—the scaffolding that lets AI take action—will become its new secret weapon, replacing even the language model itself as the connective tissue across all products.

Logan Kilpatrick runs Google AI Studio and the Gemini API. He's been at the center of Google's AI push, and he just dropped a contrarian take that flips conventional wisdom: AI models were the first wave. Now it's the *harness* that matters.

Historically, Gemini was Google's through-line—the same model powering Search, Gmail, and Cloud. That's old news. The new through-line is Antigravity, Google's agent framework. The same harness that lets developers build coding agents now powers action across Search, the Gemini app, and AI Studio. Kilpatrick puts it bluntly: "Coding has proved to be the general purpose agent harness in addition to also working really well for coding."

Here's the counterintuitive part: nobody at Google seems worried about agents eating their own lunch. Critics argue that autonomous agents doing your emails means fewer eyeballs on Google products. Kilpatrick disagrees. "At the beginning of the current AI era, everyone assumed that AI being able to answer questions for you was going to be negative for search. And actually, what's ended up happened is it's an incredibly positive sum for search. People are searching more."

He frames it clearly: "Success for Google probably doesn't look like maximizing eyeball time in front of our products. It's like maximizing outcomes for customers to do the thing that they wanna do so that they can go and live their life."

On the maturity curve, Google's suite is still "crawling"—mostly cautious, human-in-the-loop experiences. The Gemini app and Antigravity are walking. Enterprise deployments? Still mostly talk. Coding agents are the only place agents are genuinely working today, though Kilpatrick believes the pattern will generalize.

His bet: the ecosystem gets more specialized, not less. A thousand focused products, not one magical general-purpose interface.
