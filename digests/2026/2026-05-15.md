### Mikey Shulman (Founder & CEO, Suno) — Podcast: Training Data

**The Takeaway:** The real disruption in AI music isn't "a better Spotify" — it's that 90% of Suno's users create something on any given day, not to publish or monetize, but for the pure joy and fulfillment of being creative. The creation itself is the entertainment.

Suno CEO Mikey Shulman dropped out of a Harvard physics PhD to build what is now the largest AI music company in the world. His origin story defies every obvious career path — but his logic is airtight: the intersection of two things that don't usually play together is a massive opportunity, whether that's quantum mechanics and low-temperature microwave engineering or music and technology.

On why music generation is NOT a scale problem: unlike LLMs where benchmarks and scale drive progress, music has no right answers. The models are actually quite small because audio is "unwieldy" — it doesn't come in discrete bits like text. Suno's breakthrough was treating everything as raw sound (48,000 samples per second, float32) rather than modeling musical knowledge explicitly. "If you tell the model there are 12 tones, it will only ever produce those 12 tones. If you tell the model there are 200 instruments, those are the only sounds you'll ever make — and you won't get the next Skrillex using Suno."

His favorite examples of what users are actually making: trap with a sitar in it, country with 808s, microtonal music — genres that have "no business going together" but emerge naturally when you're not constraining the model with human music theory.

On Warner Music partnership: McDermott's philosophy applies here too. "It would be a real shame if there were two worlds of music, an AI world and a non-AI world. Most music will have some AI in it, just like most music today is auto-tuned or digitally produced." The goal isn't to replace the music industry — it's to build things together that could never have existed before.

The insight that cuts deepest: "You're not going to want to doom scroll after all the robots do our work. We're going to want to be productive and fulfilled." And Suno is the proof of concept.

**Suno CEO Mikey Shulman 认为，AI音乐真正的突破不是"更好听的 Spotify"，而是让90%的用户从消费者变成创作者。** 他从哈佛物理PhD退学创办 Suno，逻辑很简单：两个不常结合的领域交叉处，是最大机会所在。Suno的 breakthrough 是把一切声音都当作原始波形（每秒48000次采样）而非用人类音乐理论去约束模型，因此能产生"根本不该在一起"的融合流派。他最深刻的洞察："doomscrolling结束后你感觉空虚，但创作让你感到充实和满足"——Suno 就是证明。

### Anthropic Engineering: New in Claude Managed Agents

**Claude Managed Agents 发布三大更新：Dreaming、Outcomes 与多Agent编排**

Three major capabilities dropped for developers building with Claude Managed Agents:

**Dreaming** (research preview): A scheduled process that reviews agent sessions and memory stores, extracts patterns, and curates memories so agents improve over time. Unlike single-session memory, dreaming surfaces patterns across multiple sessions — recurring mistakes, workflows that converge on, team preferences. It restructures memory to stay high-signal as it evolves. Harvey using this saw completion rates go up ~6x.

**Outcomes**: Write a rubric describing what success looks like. A separate grader evaluates output against your criteria in its own context window (so it isn't influenced by the agent's reasoning). When something isn't right, the grader pinpoints what needs to change and the agent takes another pass. In testing: up to +10 points task success on the hardest problems, +8.4% on docx files, +10.1% on pptx.

**Multiagent orchestration**: A lead agent breaks the job into pieces and delegates to specialists, each with its own model, prompt, and tools. Specialists work in parallel on a shared filesystem. Events are persistent and every agent remembers what it's done. Full trace in the Claude Console for visibility.

**Anthropic 官方博客发布 Claude Managed Agents 三大更新：**

**Dreaming**（研究预览）：跨 Session 提取模式并自动更新记忆，Harvey 使用后完成率提升约6倍。**Outcomes**：定义成功标准，单独 grader 在独立 context 中评分并指引 Agent 修正，实测最难任务成功率提升10点。**多Agent编排**：主Agent将任务分配给专业子Agent并行处理，全流程在 Claude Console 中可追溯。

### Training Data: Suno's Mikey Shulman — Everyone Can Make Music Now

**The Takeaway:** The real disruption in AI music isn't "a better Spotify" — it's that 90% of Suno's users create something on any given day, not to publish or monetize, but for the pure joy and fulfillment of being creative. Mikey Shulman dropped out of a Harvard physics PhD to build the largest AI music company in the world, and this conversation is a masterclass in product intuition, contrarian thinking, and what it means to build something that "wasn't possible until today."

The conversation's most memorable exchange: Mikey was asked about the word "slop" — the pejorative applied to AI-generated content. His response: "I made two songs with my five-year-old yesterday. Is that slop? In the sense that 99.999% of the planet has no interest in hearing that? Sure. But that's really meaningful to me." This is the crux of his entire philosophy. Music isn't about optimizing for theBillboard charts — it's about the deeply human act of creating something personal and the fulfillment that comes from it.

On why Suno focused on full songs with lyrics rather than background music: "A song is a story and captivates you in a way that vocal-less background music just won't." They chose autoregressive generation over diffusion — meaning the audio wasn't always crisp early on, and every competitor had cleaner sound. But they stuck with it because "making a song and telling a story is more important than making crisp audio."

His boldest prediction: within 12 months, you'll see a truly interactive concert where the audience co-creates music with the artist in real time. "Why does group music-making have to be confined only to a religious context? Why can't that happen at Coachella?"

**Training Data 播客中，Suno CEO Mikey Shulman 说："我昨天和我5岁的孩子一起做了两首歌，那是 slop 吗？全世界99.999%的人对那两首歌没兴趣——是的。但这对我非常有意义。"** 这就是他整个哲学的核心：音乐不是优化排行榜，而是创作和个人满足感。他选择用自回归模型而非扩散模型，即使早期音质不够清晰，因为"讲故事的歌曲比音质清晰的背景音乐更重要"。他预测12个月内会出现真正的互动演唱会，观众与艺术家实时共同创作音乐。
