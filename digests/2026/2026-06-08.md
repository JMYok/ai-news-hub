### Madhu Guru (Former Product Lead, Google Gemini & Veo)

**English:**
Former Google product leader Madhu Guru outlined the three-phase evolution of enterprise model routing — and why Phase 3 is the most exciting. Phase 1 (2024): everyone defaulted to the "it" model (GPT), chasing the shiny new thing regardless of task. Phase 2 (early 2025): teams over-corrected, seeking the smallest/cheapest model for every job but lacking eval infrastructure to map tasks to models — resulting in wasted cycles and slower shipping. Phase 3 (now): the most sophisticated AI-native startups succeeded by breaking products into sub-agents, routing each task to the right model — hardest reasoning to Claude, simplest to Gemini Flash-Lite or open-weight models. Enterprises are now following the AI-native builders with a 6-9 month lag. The core insight: good evals + model diversity = the ability to build a routing layer that's a genuine competitive moat.

### Aaron Levie (CEO, Box)

**English:**
Box CEO Aaron Levie made the case that token costs are becoming the defining enterprise AI conversation — and that's actually bullish for the industry. When token costs become a significant portion of any workflow, it creates a new form of differentiation at the applied AI layer: model routing. Frontier intelligence will always be relevant for high-end tasks (coding, legal, financial, healthcare), and spending there will only grow. But equally, you can peel off individual tasks to lower-cost models — whether open-weight or from major labs — and deliver a more efficient end outcome. The companies that win will be those that understand their domain's workflows better than anyone else, have the best evals, and can route workloads optimally. "If you're doing document extraction, you need to know which models perform better for any given document type. If you're doing legal analysis, you want to know which models perform various types of tasks best." This will become one of the bigger differentiation points over time.

### Unsupervised Learning — Ep 89: AI Research Legend's Honest Assessment of Where We Are

**English:**
Lucas Kaiser, the original transformer paper co-author with roles at both Google and OpenAI, gave the most technically honest podcast conversation of the year. He started with the generalization question: current transformers + reasoning + agents can do amazing things — but they learn concepts "after exhausting all other options." You need a trillion tokens to learn what humans grasp from a handful of examples, and that doesn't feel like how human learning works. His working hypothesis: there is something else that could generalize much better, possibly with a different form of understanding — but every time you try to pin it down, it evaporates or the transformer catches up.

On coding agents specifically: he reproduced a lost-code paper in two days that used to take three weeks — a 10x efficiency gain. But more than speed, it changed his cognitive load. He no longer needs to hold every class name and function in his head — he can think at the ML level (loss, architecture) while agents handle implementation. He feels sharper, not less sharp. On the post-transformer landscape: there's a real feeling that we haven't exploited everything in machine learning. Small models like TRM and HRM do surprisingly well on formal reasoning tasks, and merging recurrence with transformers is an active area. But will it generalize to language at scale? The next few years will tell.

On Anthropic being first to succeed at coding: they made a deliberate decision to focus on coding when OpenAI was all-in on ChatGPT. "They just could not compete" in chat, so they bet on something else — and it worked. On multimodal: current approaches feel fundamentally misaligned with how humans process the world. We take in all sensory input in parallel, at massive bandwidth. "We shouldn't be putting these tiny patches in, which should just go in, be processed somehow." The real multimodal breakthrough hasn't happened yet.

**中文：**
Lucas Kaiser，原始 transformer 论文合著者，曾在 Google 和 OpenAI 任职，进行了今年技术上最坦诚的播客对话。他从泛化问题开始：当前的 transformer + reasoning + agent 可以做惊人的事情——但它们学习概念的方式是"穷尽所有其他选项之后"。你需要一万亿个 token 才能学到人类从少数样本就能理解的概念，这感觉不像人类学习的方式。他的工作假设：确实有其他东西可以泛化得更好，可能具有不同形式的理解——但每次你试图准确定位它时，它就蒸发了，或者 transformer 追了上来。

关于 coding agents：他用两天复现了一篇丢失代码的论文，以前需要三周——10 倍效率提升。但比速度更重要的是，它改变了他的认知负担。他不再需要将每个类名和函数记在脑中——他可以在 ML 层面（损失、架构）思考，而 agent 处理实现。他感觉自己更敏锐了，而不是更迟钝。关于后 transformer 格局：有真实的感受是，我们还没有挖掘机器学习中的一切。像 TRM 和 HRM 这样的小模型在形式推理任务上表现惊人，将递归与 transformer 结合是一个活跃领域。但它会泛化到大规模语言吗？未来几年将会揭晓。

关于 Anthropic 最先在 coding 上取得成功：他们做出了有意的决定，在 OpenAI 全押 ChatGPT 时专注于 coding。"他们在 chat 上就是竞争不过"，所以他们赌了别的东西——结果奏效了。关于多模态：当前方法感觉与人类处理世界的方式根本不对齐。我们并行处理所有感官输入，以巨大的带宽。"我们不应该把这些小块塞进去，应该直接进去，以某种方式处理。"真正的多模态突破还没有发生。
