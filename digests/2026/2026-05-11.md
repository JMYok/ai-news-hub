### Aaron Levie (CEO, Box)

**AI is leveling the field — but experts still have the edge. And that will always be true.**

Box CEO Aaron Levie pushed back on the "democratization means experts are obsolete" narrative: agents do make it easier to enter complex fields, but experienced practitioners consistently outperform novices with agents because they know when the agent is making a catastrophic mistake, and can give it the right context to actually do the job well.

His point cuts both ways: quality and volume of output will go up for everyone. But the person with experience using agents will always beat the person using agents alone. "You'll trust a lawyer using an agent over someone who's never experienced how well a contract holds up. You'll rely on the instincts of a designer using agents over the average prompter."

Box CEO Aaron Levie 反驳了"AI 民主化=专家过时"的叙事：专家+Agent 的组合始终超越单独使用 Agent 的新手。老手知道 Agent 什么时候在犯致命错误，能给出正确上下文让 Agent 真正做好工作。他的结论是：输出质量会提升，但有经验的人始终领先。"你会信任一个使用 Agent 的律师，而不是从没经历过合同是否经得住检验的人。"

### Peter Yang (Product, Roblox)

**The slop compounding problem: how AI-generated markdown slowly poisons your codebase.**

Roblox Product's Peter Yang highlighted a trap he's watching unfold in real time: teams start by using AI to generate some markdown files, skim them because they "look ok," and don't bother editing manually. Then they generate more files — which now reference the previous slop. Over time, 5% slop becomes 10%, then 20%, until you've got a pile of AI-generated content that no one understands and feels impossible to clean up.

This is a real architectural risk that isn't discussed enough. The compound effect of low-quality AI generation, without human editing discipline, is technical debt at scale.

Roblox 产品负责人 Peter Yang 指出了一个被忽视的陷阱：AI 生成的 markdown 文件如果不经人工审核，slop 会指数级累积。开始是 5%，然后 10%、20%，最终变成一堆没人知道怎么工作的 AI 垃圾，却没有清理的头绪。技术债务会随着 AI 生成规模化而规模化。

### Anthropic Engineering: April 23 Postmortem

**Three separate bugs caused the "Claude Code degradation" reports. All fixed. Usage limits reset.**

Anthropic Engineering published a detailed postmortem on the wave of user reports about Claude Code quality issues. The key finding: three independent changes, each affecting a different slice of traffic on a different schedule, created the aggregate appearance of broad, inconsistent degradation.

1. **March 4**: Changed default reasoning effort from `high` to `medium` to reduce long tail latency. User feedback was negative — they wanted default to higher intelligence. Reverted April 7. Affected Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6.

2. **March 26**: A caching optimization meant to clear old thinking after 1 hour of inactivity had a bug — instead of clearing once, it cleared on every turn for the rest of the session. Claude became forgetful and repetitive. Fixed April 10. Affected Sonnet 4.6 and Opus 4.6.

3. **April 16**: Added a system prompt instruction ("keep text between tool calls ≤25 words, final responses ≤100 words") to reduce verbosity. In combination with other changes, it hurt coding quality. Reverted April 20. Affected Sonnet 4.6, Opus 4.6, and Opus 4.7.

Notable: when back-testing the offending changes with Opus 4.7 and full repository context, Opus 4.7 found the caching bug. Opus 4.6 didn't. Anthropic is now shipping improved Code Review to customers and adding tighter controls on system prompt changes.

Anthropic Engineering 发布了关于 Claude Code 质量问题的完整复盘：三个独立 bug 导致了用户感知的"模型降级。（1）3月4日将默认 reasoning effort 从 high 改为 medium，已回滚；（2）3月26日的缓存优化 bug 导致 thinking 被每轮持续清除，已修复；（3）4月16日的 system prompt 限制输出长度，影响编码质量，已回滚。用 Opus 4.7 配合完整仓库上下文回测时，Opus 4.7 能找到第二个 bug，Opus 4.6 做不到。所有订阅者使用限制已重置。

### Training Data: ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski — Voice as the Interface for Everything

**The Takeaway:** ElevenLabs has built the full audio stack — TTS, STT, dubbing, voice engine, music — and is now betting on emotional intelligence as the next frontier. The most overlooked opportunities for voice agents aren't customer support; they're citizen services, education, and healthcare. The current bottleneck isn't capability, it's emotional interaction quality and speed.

ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski joined Training Data for a wide-ranging conversation covering the company's origin (childhood friends frustrated that Polish TV dubbed everything with one monotone voice), to building a frontier audio company in 2022 when everyone else was focused on crypto and metaverse.

On the business: ElevenLabs generates north of $100M ARR with just over 400 people. The key operating principle that enabled this: keep teams extremely small (cap under 10 people for each research/product team), put engineers inside non-technical teams (legal, people ops, go-to-market), and maintain no titles so people can optimize for impact rather than hierarchy.

On voice agents: customer support is the well-known use case. The overlooked opportunities are citizen services (Ukraine's government deployed a voice agent so citizens can call in for frontline information and educational content), education (imagine learning physics from a Feynman voice tutor on your headphones, or cooking with Gordon Ramsay shouting at you in real time), and healthcare.

The next frontier Staniszewski is most excited about: **emotional intelligence in voice agents** — not just getting the right intonation, but understanding when the caller is stressed and responding with a soothing tone, matching excitement when the caller is excited, slowing down when someone speaks slowly. That emotional intelligence path is "finally seeing internally a path to solving."

Current bottlenecks: real emotional interaction still doesn't work well enough, latency is too high, and music generation can't yet produce Top Charts-quality music.

Training Data 播客：ElevenLabs CEO Mati Staniszewski 分享了声音 AI 的现状与未来。公司起源很人性——两个波兰长大的朋友受不了波兰电视用单一声音给所有内容配音。现状：400多人，ARR超过1亿美元，核心原则是保持团队极小（每个研究/产品团队不到10人），在非技术团队里放工程师，保持无头衔文化。

关于语音 Agent：最被忽视的机会不是客服，而是公民服务（乌克兰政府已部署语音 Agent 供公民获取前线信息）、教育（想象用法曼物理导师的声音学物理，或者让 Gordon Ramsay 实时教你烹饪）和医疗。下一阶段最大的突破点是**情感智能语音 Agent**——能感知对方情绪并传递对应情绪。这条路"终于看到内部解决方案"。现状瓶颈：真正的情感交互还不够好，速度太慢，音乐还无法达到 Top Charts 水准。
