### AI & I by Every: GitHub's COO Explains Why AI Hasn't Replaced Developers

GitHub COO Kyle Daigle sits down for a wide-ranging conversation on what's actually happening at the frontier of AI-assisted coding—and the numbers are staggering. GitHub is on track for 14 billion commits this year, up from 1 billion last year. More striking: in March alone, AI agents submitted 17 million pull requests. That's just agents. And yet Daigle pushes back hard on the "it's all slop" narrative—his argument is that we're entering an era where it's not just one developer building, but one developer plus a team of agents, all leveraging the same context and resources.

Daigle's most candid admission: he uses three different machines daily (Mac, Windows, Linux) because he refuses to be a prisoner of any one platform's agent experience. He uses the GitHub Copilot app on Windows specifically to make sure Windows developers get equal treatment. On the "$200 to $2,000 subscription" problem, he believes the answer is model routing—automatically stepping down from GPT-5.5 to Haiku for simple find-and-replace tasks without you having to think about it. His personal agent loop is equally revealing: he feeds it all his emails and Slack messages and gets a weekly "Kyle, you keep saying this and it's unclear" report. Self-improvement via AI, recursively.

**The Takeaway:** The real bottleneck isn't code generation—it's the manual review, merge, and change management loop that still requires human judgment. The companies that win will be the ones that make that entire circle smoother, not just the generation step.

### Aaron Levie (Box CEO)

Levie published his most systematic thinking yet on why the Applied AI layer is more defensible than critics think. His four-part thesis: (1) The moat isn't the LLM—it's the workflow-specific features that capture the right data and provide the right bespoke tools for agents to use. (2) Model routing is a natural advantage for neutral platforms—they can business-model-alignedly use whatever intelligence level a task actually needs. (3) FDEs (Field Development Engineers) are doing the actual change management work that makes enterprise AI stick. (4) Domain-specific GTM creates compounding expertise that generalized players can't replicate. His contrarian bet: "Most aspects of how you bring intelligence to real world work today remain important no matter how good the models get."

https://x.com/levie/status/2067455756795039957

Levie 发布了迄今为止最系统性的思考，论证为什么 Applied AI 层比批评者认为的更具防御性。他的四部分论点：(1) 护城河不是 LLM——而是那些能捕获正确数据、为 agent 提供正确定制工具的workflow特定功能。(2) 模型路由是中立平台的天然优势——它们可以从商业模型层面决定某个任务需要多少智能。(3) FDE（现场开发工程师）正在做让企业 AI 真正落地的实际变革管理工作。(4) 领域特定的 GTM 建立了泛化玩家无法复制的复合专业知识。他的反向赌注："今天你把智能带入现实世界工作的大多数方面，无论模型变得多好，都会继续重要。"

### Garry Tan (YC President & CEO)

Garry Tan published a striking back-of-envelope calculation on the cost of a hypothetical Fable 5 ban: roughly $12M per working hour in lost productivity. His math: 5M AI-coding developers, $90/hr fully-loaded cost, 17.8% of work routed to Fable in 48 hours, Fable ~15% more productive. "2.7% × $90/hr = ~$2.40/dev/hr × 5M devs = $12M per working hour." A vivid illustration of what frontier model access actually means economically.
