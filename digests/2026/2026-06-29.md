## How we contain Claude across products

Anthropic has shifted from rejecting AI agent deployments to routinely granting them significant access, driven by productivity gains. They now focus on "containment" (sandboxes, VMs, egress controls) rather than purely human-in-the-loop supervision, since users approve 93% of prompts and become less diligent over time. Security risks fall into three categories: user misuse, model misbehavior, and external threats. The company maintains different containment architectures for different products (claude.ai, Claude Code, Claude Cowork), accepting that some residual risk will always remain as capabilities expand.

### The GPU Myth: State of AI Compute 2026 | Stephen Balaban

**The Takeaway:** GPU compute isn't a commodity—it's a complex, vertically integrated business, and the AI infrastructure boom is just getting started.

If you've been hearing that AI cloud services would commoditize and GPU rental prices would crash, Stephen Balaban, cofounder and CTO of Lambda, wants to set the record straight. Lambda is one of the leading "neo clouds" powering the AI revolution, and Balaban has a front-row seat to the insatiable demand for compute. His message: you're underestimating how big this is going to get.

The core misconception? People assumed cloud compute works like selling electricity—same product, competing on price. Balaban argues it's actually more like building a skyscraper: "The big thing is that cloud compute is not a commodity service. It is a very complicated, highly vertically integrated type of service that spans everything from land entitlement, construction, HPC design, software, virtualization, cloud services on top."

Here's what's actually happening in the market: long-term GPU rental rates are holding steady or increasing, despite what some indices suggest. The methodology gets skewed by contract mix. Meanwhile, scaling laws—the principle that more compute yields smarter models—show no signs of plateauing. As AI expands from "better search" to "software engineering substitute," the addressable market keeps growing.

The real bottleneck isn't GPUs or financing—it's entitled land with guaranteed power. Building data centers requires navigating local communities, utilities, and years of entitlement work. That's why Lambda focuses on innovation across every layer: their one-click cluster software lets customers spin up 16 to 4,000 GPUs instantly, something most competitors can't match.

Bottom line: This market has room for multiple winners, and we're still early.
