### Oriol Vinyals · Google DeepMind
**Co-lead of Gemini**

The last frontier of AI isn't what you think. In a wide-ranging interview recorded post-Google I/O, Oriol Vinyals — co-lead of Gemini alongside Jeff Dean — pulled back the curtain on what Google actually believes about World Models, agent memory, and the true shape of progress. His most honest moment: when asked whether video and images have had their "GPT moment," he said flatly: "Probably not." That's a significant admission from someone inside the lab building the most multimodal system in the world.

His framing of World Models is specific and useful: language is the control interface, but the actual world simulator underneath still needs to operate at a precision level we haven't cracked — the exact force needed to grasp an object, the tactile feel of surfaces. The robotics applications are obvious, but the gap between "looks right" and "works right" is still enormous.

On agent memory, Vinyals made a point that cuts against the prevailing narrative: external knowledge bases — the file system approach — are more practical than writing knowledge back into model weights, because you can't serve a different model to each user at scale. This is already working in production at Harvey and similar operations.

**Key quote:**
> "I don't think I've seen truly outstanding ideas that a model has generated yet, but I am sure I will very soon because there are insights the models have access to a bandwidth of information we don't."

(That said: he suspects the models understand their own training mechanics in ways that are superhuman, precisely because of that information bandwidth.)

He also framed the most useful mental model for evaluating model intelligence different from how we'd expect — not benchmarks, but new environments: play a new video game you've never seen trained on, from an instruction manual alone. Most frontier models still fail this.

### Oriol Vinyals · Google DeepMind
**Gemini 联合负责人**

AI 的最后前沿并不是你想的那样。Google I/O 后录制的这期访谈中，Oriol Vinyals——与 Jeff Dean 并肩的 Gemini 联合负责人——罕见地撕开了 Google 对 World Model、Agent 记忆和真正进步形态的真实看法。他最诚实的一刻：当被直接问及视频/图像是否已经来了「GPT 时刻」，他坦率地说：「还没真正到来。」这对于身处全球最强大多模态系统内部的人来说，是相当重要的承认。

他对 World Model 的定义很精准：语言是控制它的界面，但底层的世界模拟器仍然需要精确到我们尚未达到的精度——精确到抓起物体所需的力度、表面的触感。机器人应用的方向是清晰的，但从「看起来对」到「真的对」的差距仍然巨大。

关于 Agent 记忆，Vinyals 点出了与主流叙事相悖的一点：外部知识库（文件系统方案）比把知识写回模型权重更实用——因为你没法给每个用户托管不同版本的模型。这套方案在 Harvey 这样的团队里已经实际运转了。

**核心引用：**
> 「模型生成的真正杰出想法我还没见过，但我确信很快会看到——因为这些模型对我们不具备的带宽信息有着访问权限。」

（他同时指出，模型对自己的训练机制的理解方式，由于这种信息带宽，可能已经是超人的。）

他还提出了一个评估智能的新思路——不是跑分，而是从一本说明书开始玩一款从未被训练过的全新视频游戏。大多数前沿模型仍然会在这里失败。

### Anthropic Engineering · New in Claude Managed Agents: dreaming, outcomes, and multiagent orchestration

Three meaningful updates to Claude Managed Agents worth tracking:

**Dreaming** is a scheduled process where agents review their own sessions, surface patterns, and update memory between runs. Unlike explicit recall, dreaming works across sessions and can identify the kinds of recurring mistakes and workflow patterns that single agents miss on their own. Harvey is using this: their completion rates went up ~6x. It's self-improvement as a feature.

**Outcomes** is a rubric-based evaluation layer. You write what "good" looks like as a description, a separate grader evaluates the agent's output against it in isolation, and the agent self-corrects until the output meets the bar. In testing, outcomes improved task success by up to 10 percent over a standard prompting loop, with the largest gains on the hardest problems. File generation quality also improved: +8.4% on docx, +10.1% on pptx. This is not marginal — it's a different architecture for quality control.

**Multiagent Orchestration**: a lead agent can now break work into pieces and delegate to specialist subagents — each with its own model, prompt, and tools — working in parallel on a shared filesystem. The lead agent can check back mid-workflow because events are persistent and every agent remembers what it's done. Netflix's platform team uses this to process logs from hundreds of builds in parallel and surface only the patterns worth acting on. Spiral by Every runs a lead agent on Haiku that fields requests and delegates drafting to Opus subagents in parallel, with outcomes enforcing editorial quality.

### Anthropic Engineering · Claude Managed Agents 新功能：Dreaming、Outcomes 与多 Agent 编排

Claude Managed Agents 三个值得关注的更新：

**Dreaming** 是一个定期运行的过程，agent 回顾自己的会话、提炼规律、在两次运行之间更新记忆。不同于显式召回，Dreaming 跨会话运行，能识别单个 agent 靠自己发现不了的反复错误和工作流模式。Harvey 在用这个：他们的任务完成率提升了约 6 倍。这就是把「自我改进」做成了功能。

**Outcomes** 是一个基于评分标准的评估层：你把「好」的定义写出来，一个独立的评判者在独立上下文里对输出评分，agent 根据反馈自我修正，直到输出达到标准。测试中，Outcomes 在标准提示循环基础上将任务成功率提升最多 10 个百分点，最困难的问题收益最大。文件生成质量也有提升：docx 提升 +8.4%，pptx 提升 +10.1%。这不是边际改进——这是一种全新的质量控制架构。

**多 Agent 编排**：主 agent 现在可以把工作拆解并发派给专家子 agent——每个子 agent 有自己的模型、提示词和工具——在共享文件系统上并行工作。主 agent 可以在中途回头检查，因为事件是持久化的，每个 agent 都记得自己做了什么。Netflix 平台团队用这个并行处理数百个构建的日志，只提炼出值得行动的规律。Every 旗下 Spiral 用 Haiku 做主 agent 接收请求，用 Opus 子 agent 并行处理起草任务，用 Outcomes 强制编辑质量标准。

*https://claude.com/blog/new-in-claude-managed-agents*

### Unsupervised Learning · Ep 87: Gemini Co-Lead on World Models, RL's Next Domains & Continual Learning

**Title:** Ep 87: Gemini Co-Lead on World Models, RL's Next Domains & Continual Learning
**Guest:** Oriol Vinyals, Co-lead of Gemini, Google DeepMind
**URL:** https://youtube.com/watch?v=NQczevdpxq0

**The Takeaway:** Google DeepMind's Oriol Vinyals argues that the real frontier isn't just making models bigger — it's solving the unsolved problems in multimodal learning (extracting concept knowledge from raw video without language supervision), cracking agent memory as a file-system-as-knowledge-base paradigm, and finding RL's next scaling domains beyond coding and math. He's candid: he doesn't think we've seen the "GPT moment" for video/images yet, World Models are still more renderer than simulator for robotics, and the path forward requires both better eval design and accepting that some foundational research questions won't pay off in the next training run.

The conversation ranged across: the philosophical difference between "World Model" as representation learning vs. as a controllable world simulator; the precision gap in robotics that makes world models still insufficient; why memory-as-filesystem beats memory-as-weights from a serving perspective; how Google balances exploration vs. exploitation across research timelines; why we should believe in broad distribution training but shouldn't bet against narrow RL causing unexpected generalization; and the eval he actually uses: novel video games from an instruction manual alone, which still flummox most models.

**Key quote:**
> "Probably what I would characterize as the GPT moment of video and images... I'm not sure we quite have seen that."

And:
> "The ability to learn from experience very efficiently... I call these meta capabilities. These are capabilities that I can sort of measure or feel, and probably it's not super good yet."
