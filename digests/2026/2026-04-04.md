### Andrej Karpathy — LLM Knowledge Bases and the Future of AI Workflows

**Andrej Karpathy（AI 研究者，前 Tesla AI 总监，OpenAI 创始团队成员）**

Karpathy 分享了他目前使用 LLM 的核心工作流：用 LLM 构建个人知识库来管理研究资料。他将源文档（文章、论文、代码库、数据集、图片）存入 raw/ 目录，然后让 LLM 逐步编译成一个 wiki——以 .md 文件存储，包含摘要、反向链接、概念分类和相互引用。使用 Obsidian 作为 IDE 前端，LLM 负责撰写和维护所有 wiki 内容，人很少直接编辑。他的 wiki 目前约 100 篇文章、近 40 万词，足够支撑复杂的问答和自主研究。

在另一条推文中，Karpathy 展望了更远的图景：未来每次向前沿 LLM 提问都会触发一整个 LLM 团队来自动化整个研究流程——迭代构建临时 wiki、lint、循环数次，最后输出完整报告，这已经远超简单的 `.decode()`。

Andrej Karpathy shared his core LLM workflow: using LLMs to build personal knowledge bases for research material. He stores source documents (articles, papers, repos, datasets, images) in a raw/ directory, then uses an LLM to incrementally "compile" a wiki—stored as .md files with summaries, backlinks, concept categorization and cross-references. He uses Obsidian as the IDE frontend, with the LLM writing and maintaining all wiki content—humans rarely edit directly. His current wiki contains ~100 articles and ~400K words, sufficient for complex Q&A and autonomous research.

In another tweet, Karpathy envisioned a grander future: every question to a frontier LLM could spawn an entire team of LLMs to automate the whole research process—iteratively constructing an ephemeral wiki, linting it, looping a few times, then writing a full report. Well beyond a simple `.decode()`.

### Aaron Levie — The Brutal Architecture Loop of Building AI Agents

**Aaron Levie（Box CEO）**

Box CEO Aaron Levie 总结了他构建 AI Agent 一年以来的核心教训：必须极度"不动感情"地对待架构。随着模型能力快速提升，你之前为 Agent 构建的许多 scaffolding 会变得多余甚至有害——但你必须主动拆除它们才能获得新能力的性能收益。

他的 rough loop 是：1) 用 LLM 构建系统确保 Agent 完成特定任务 → 2) 模型能力大幅提升，许多系统变得冗余 → 3) 移除旧的 scaffolding 以获得新性能 → 4) 新能力出现 → 5) 回到步骤 1。在 Box Agent 的案例中，从最初架构设计到最终发布期间，由于模型在复杂推理、搜索工具使用、动态代码生成、context window 准确性等方面的飞速进步，他们不得不演进多个组件——许多原本用来处理 context window 限制的 mitigation 反而在模型变强后降低了质量。

Box CEO Aaron Levie distilled his core lesson from a year of building AI agents: you have to be brutally unsentimental about your architecture. As model capabilities improve rapidly, much of the scaffolding you built around your agent becomes redundant or even harmful—but you must actively remove it to capture the new performance gains.

His rough loop: 1) Build systems around the LLM to ensure the agent solves specific tasks well → 2) Model capabilities dramatically improve, making many of those systems redundant → 3) Remove prior scaffolding to get new performance gains → 4) New capabilities emerge → 5) Return to step 1. In the Box Agent case, from original architecture to final release, they had to evolve multiple components as models dramatically improved at complex reasoning, tool use, on-the-fly code generation, and context window accuracy—many mitigations they had put in place for context window limitations actually started producing lower quality results as models got better.

### Ryo Lu — Cursor 3 and the Philosophy of "Glass Over Black Box"

**Ryo Lu（Cursor Designer）**

Cursor 设计师 Ryo Lu 发布了 Cursor 3，同时阐述了他对 AI 工具哲学的核心观点："Glass over Black Box"。他认为 Terminal 是一个黑盒——一行进，一行出，你学会像机器一样思考。AI 保持了黑盒但让它更上瘾了：你输入一个愿望，拉下杠杆，得到结果，接受或拒绝整个产物，你看到越来越少、思考越来越少。

而 Glass 的理念是：PM 写出计划并看着它变成现实；设计师画出草图并实时在代码中迭代；工程师在写任何代码前先分解系统架构；新手阅读每一个 diff、提问、学习、建立直觉。Agent 是可见的，diff 是存在的，计划是可编辑的，状态是清晰的。你可以在任何时候 Tab 进文件进行编辑。Nothing hides. Nothing is forced.

Cursor designer Ryo Lu launched Cursor 3 while articulating his core philosophy for AI tools: "Glass over Black Box." He argues the terminal was a black box—one line in, few lines out—you learned to think like the machine. AI kept the black box and made it more addictive: you type a wish, pull the lever, something comes out, you accept or reject the whole thing, you see less and think less as the model got better.

Glass breaks the box to fit you: the PM writes a plan and watches it become real; the designer sketches an idea and iterates live in code; the engineer breaks down systems architecture before a single line is written; the new programmer reads every diff, asks questions, learns, builds intuition. The agents are visible, the diff is there, the plan is editable, the state is clear. You can Tab and make edits in files when you need to. Nothing hides. Nothing is forced.

### Training Data — How Autonomous Labs Will Transform Scientific Research

**Jason Kelly（Ginkgo Bioworks 创始人兼 CEO）**

Ginkgo Bioworks CEO Jason Kelly 在 Training Data 播客中描绘了生物自动化的未来图景，与主持人 Sonya 和 Duncan 深度探讨了 AI + 机器人实验室如何从根本上加速科学发现。

**核心观点：AI + 自动化实验室 = 科学发现的加速器**

Kelly 认为 AI 在科学领域的变革性在于两个维度。第一是"组合性优势"：如果你有 100 个 AI 科学家同时追求不同的阿尔茨海默病假设，它们每天共享实验数据——你失败的实验结果可能对我的假设有用，这在传统学术体系中永远不会发生。第二是"成本结构重构"：当前科研经费只有不到 5% 真正花在试剂上，绝大部分是人工和实验室空间成本。如果 AI 驱动的自动化实验室能实现制造业级别的利用率（从目前低于 20% 提升到 70%+），科学发现的成本结构将彻底改变。

他特别强调这与过去 30 年科技革命的不同：互联网、社交媒体对生物科技毫无影响——它们只是"back office IT"。但 AI + 自动化实验室不同，"这将真正改变我们做科学的基础"。

**关于 AI 能否真正做科学实验**

Ginkgo 与 OpenAI 的合作实验回答了这个问题：他们给 AI 模型提供了细胞自由蛋白质合成（cell free synthesis）的优化任务，让 AI 设计实验、人类机器人执行、结果返回给 AI、再设计。经过 6 轮迭代，他们超越了斯坦福 Mike Jewett 团队保持的最先进水平 40%。关键在于：AI 并非更聪明，而是能 24/7 不间断运行实验，而且能系统性地从每个失败中学习。

**关于类人机器人**

Kelly 直言不看好类人机器人在实验室的应用："在实验室里，人类做的最主要事情是移动样本。但有比用 bipedal 在设备间走动好得多的方式来解决这个问题。我们用轨道系统，样本以微米精度移动，比人可靠得多。"

**关于生物编程的未来应用**

Kelly 提到当前生物编程的实际应用几乎 100% 是药物发现（therapeutics），这让他感到"尴尬"。但他看到两条出路：1) GLP-1 药物（如 Ozempic）证明消费者愿意为"健康"而非"疾病治疗"付大价钱；2) 如果普通人也能通过 AI 设计实验并远程使用自动化实验室，"每个人都可能成为科学家"。这让人想起 1960 年代有人说"以后小孩都会编程"时的嘲讽态度。

Jason Kelly, co-founder and CEO of Ginkgo Bioworks, appeared on Training Data to paint a vision of the future where AI + robotic labs fundamentally accelerate scientific discovery, in a deep conversation with hosts Sonya and Duncan.

**Core argument: AI + Autonomous Labs = Accelerant for Scientific Discovery**

Kelly believes AI's transformative impact on science operates in two dimensions. First, "compositional advantage": if you have 100 AI scientists pursuing different Alzheimer's hypotheses simultaneously, they share experimental data daily—your failed results might be relevant to my hypothesis, something that never happens in traditional academia. Second, "cost structure restructuring": currently less than 5% of research funding actually goes to reagents, the vast majority is labor and lab space. If AI-driven autonomous labs can achieve manufacturing-level utilization (from today's sub-20% to 70%+), the cost structure of scientific discovery will fundamentally change.

He emphasized this differs from the last 30 years of tech revolutions: the internet and social media had zero impact on biotech—they were just "back office IT." But AI + autonomous labs are different: "this is actually going to change the fundamentals of how we do science."

**On Whether AI Can Truly Do Scientific Experiments**

The OpenAI collaboration answered this: they gave an AI model the task of optimizing cell-free protein synthesis, letting AI design experiments, robots execute them, results feed back to AI, redesign. After 6 rounds of iteration, they beat Stanford's Mike Jewett lab's state-of-the-art by 40%. The key insight: AI isn't smarter, it's just that it can run experiments 24/7 and systematically learn from every failure.

**On Humanoid Robots in Labs**

Kelly was direct about not expecting humanoids in labs: "In the lab, the primary thing humans do is move samples around. But there are far better ways to solve that than walking bipedally between equipment. We use a track system where samples move with micron-level precision—far more reliable than humans."

**On Future Applications of Programming Biology**

Kelly noted that virtually 100% of current biological programming applications are therapeutics, which he finds "embarrassing." But he sees two exits: 1) GLP-1 drugs (like Ozempic) prove consumers will pay big for "health" not just "disease treatment"; 2) If ordinary people can design experiments via AI and use autonomous labs remotely, "everyone could become a scientist." This recalls how in the 1960s people mocked the idea that kids would someday program computers.
