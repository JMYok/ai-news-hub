### Swyx

**Frame Tasks as Questions Instead of Commands — Append "?" to Your Prompt**

Swyx shared a prompt technique: instead of commanding a model to execute, frame the task as a question so the model can push back and evaluate idea quality. Simply appending "?" to your prompt often does the trick.

> "Always frame your task as a question, so that the model is invited to push back and rate the quality of the idea/suggest alternatives, rather than blindly execute what you SAID to do (which is often not precisely what you MEANT)."

### Peter Yang

**How to Build Self-Checking and Self-Improving AI Skills: A 5-Step Framework**

Peter Yang shared a complete workflow for building self-improving AI skills: 1) Provide context with examples of good output; 2) Write clear skill descriptions using "Use when the user wants to [do this]"; 3) Add evals with 10 pass/fail checks; 4) Add memory for past learnings; 5) Build a meta-skill to clean up other skills. ### AI & I by Every — "The SaaS Apocalypse Is a Goldmine" with Figma PM Matt Colyer

**Figma's Developer Product Director on Why the SaaS Apocalypse Is a Myth — and What AI Agents Mean for Design**

Figma's Director of Product for Developers Matt Colyer sat down with Dan Shipper for a wide-ranging conversation on AI, design, and the future of software. His central thesis: the SaaS apocalypse is a myth — the number of software developers is expanding from 30M to 1B+, meaning demand for tools is exploding not shrinking.

He's been vibe coding for two years, starting with an email agent to handle his kids' school PTO emails. He's now maintained inbox zero for four consecutive weeks. On Figma's AI strategy: Figma Agent runs directly on the Infinite Canvas, Figma MCP supports external agents reading and writing designs, and the Design-to-Code pipeline is fully operational with agents creating PRs automatically.

He outlined a forward-looking framework for design + AI: divergent agents generate 25 frames in different directions, conversational agents evaluate from a user's perspective, and convergent agents cluster and prioritize. The biggest bottleneck he sees now isn't generation — it's review: "How do we scale our value system to evaluate whether the new thing we're creating is actually good?"

On the future of PMs and designers: "The people who cannot leverage these tools are the ones that just accept the output. The people who drive the next set of tools are the curious ones who push boundaries and understand how things work."

> "I think the most important thing is to be a curious person. Right? Because with these new tools, the people who cannot leverage them are the ones that just kind of accept the output."
